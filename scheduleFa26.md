## 9/10 Origins: Marx, Machines, and "the general intellect"
### readings/in class   
* Karl Marx, "Fragment on Machines" from *Grundrisse*
* Herman Melville, *Bartleby, the Scrivener*
* check out [Marx GPT](https://chatgpt.com/g/g-DfsI6piuA-karl-marx-gpt) and dialogue with it as you read the "Fragment"


### work due
* **Blog Post #1:** in the mid-19thC, Marx ruminates on the many ironies that characterize our relationship to the machine. This works along at least three major axes: a) machines enhance the productivity of labor (obviously); b) machines *embody* labor: they result from human mental/manual labor and contain, apart from whatever they produce, the sum total of a dizzing set of contributions from what Marx calls the "general intellect"; c) machines replace human labor, erode the value of once-scarce skills, and thus face workers, under capitalism at least, as a foe. Using some combination of Marx's text, Melville's novella (which depicts a machine-like human copier, 120 years before the Xerox), and Marx GPT, a newer embodiment of one corner of the "general intellect," reflect on the way machines, from the power looms of the 19thC to the GPTs of the 21st, relate to labor. Feel free to reflect on your own labor in making this post. Posts should be about 500-800 words. They can be a bit loose and speculative, and they may incorporate some first-person musings, but they should have a clear focus.
